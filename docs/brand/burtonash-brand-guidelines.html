maynard

A personal brand built from the machines that started it all: DEC's press style of the seventies and eighties, phosphor-lit terminals, and one IBM XT that never really got switched off.

This document is the single source of truth for the burtonash identity. It is written to be read by people and ingested by tools — hand BRAND.md to Claude Design, Claude Code, or anything else that builds on your behalf. The page you are reading is set entirely in the system it describes.

01

Foundations

burtonash is the personal identity of Ash — product person by day; cryptography, amateur radio, and retro computing by night. It sells nothing. It exists so that everything Ash publishes — a blog post, a lab, a LinkedIn carousel, a video end card — is recognisably one voice.

Personality

Bright, casual, professional. Confident and playful, never salesy. The tone of someone who will happily explain how a cipher breaks and why that is delightful. If a choice feels like influencer marketing, it is wrong; if it feels like a really good 1976 product brochure, it is right.

Heritage

Every colour and device in this system has a hardware story. The palette is drawn from Digital Equipment Corporation's print and panel design — the PDP-8/e faceplate's earth-and-gold register blocks, the PDP-11 brochure's deep red — plus the phosphor colours of real terminal tubes. Nothing in the system is arbitrary; that is what makes it feel inevitable rather than decorated.

Principles

  • Restraint is the style. DEC's covers were one colour field, white type, and a single framed image. When in doubt, remove something.
  • Homage, never imitation. Borrow the devices (boxed lowercase letters, register bars, keylines), never the marks themselves.
  • Lowercase is the register. digital was always lowercase; so is burtonash.
  • Accessibility is part of the aesthetic. Every text pairing in this document passes WCAG AA; the panel keylines exist because contrast rules and homage happen to agree.

03

Typography

One family, two voices: IBM Plex. It is IBM's own typeface — the XT connection is the whole joke — it is free, and it is available on Google Fonts and in Canva, so the system ports everywhere without substitution.

Roles

FaceRoleWeights
IBM Plex SansBody copy, headings, UI text400 body · 500 headings and emphasis. Nothing heavier.
IBM Plex MonoWordmark, prompts, labels, tags, code, spec tablesText (450) preferred, Medium (500) where 450 is unavailable · 400 only for long code blocks · 700 reserved for mark letters
  • Mono never runs at a weight that renders light. Regular 400 reads thin at small sizes; the working weight is Text/Medium. Self-host Plex Mono Text 450 from IBM's official release for the website; Google Fonts carries 500 as the nearest stop.
  • Never hollow. Outline or stroked display type is banned in all contexts. Headings are always solid.
  • Marks are lowercase, always. Headings and body copy use sentence case.
  • A pixel face (Departure Mono, VT323) may appear as garnish in CGA-reserve contexts only — never as body text.

Bright ideas, broken ciphers, and the occasional soldering burn

Plex Sans 500 heading over Plex Sans 400 body. Comfortable, warm, unfussy.

04

Marks

A small family rather than a single logo. Three tools, one grammar.

4.1 — Terminal prompt (primary)

The everyday mark: the prompt, set in Plex Mono Medium/Text, with a solid block cursor. Cursor colour is the surface rule made visible — red on paper, amber on dark.

ash:~$
ash:~$

Usage: site navigation (top left), footers, avatars in text contexts, sign-offs. The full-handle form burtonash:~$ is permitted where the short form is ambiguous.

4.2 — Tile mark (secondary)

The DEC homage: lowercase bold letters, one per tall tile. A secondary element — it is not primary website chrome — but a fully specified component for avatars, favicons, banners, end cards, and print.

digital red — the primary tile mark. White-warm lowercase in deep red boxes; the closest the system comes to tipping its hat directly.

ash

warm black — the quiet default for documents and letterheads. faceplate — the playful trio, in 8/e band order.

ashash

Splits — in two-colour bars, burton takes a neutral or earth colour and the ash tiles always carry the signature red (or amber in terminal contexts). The mark tells you where you are.

burtonash

warm black + red — the banner split. Also approved: brown + amber (8/e homage) and red + amber (loud; end cards only).

The cut-out rule (optional): on paper surfaces, tile letters may be set in paper #F3EEE2 itself — after the original digital logo, whose letters were designed not as white but as cut-outs showing the material beneath.

4.3 — Faceplate rendering (dark-surface banner)

The canonical dark-surface treatment: tiles mounted on a warm-black panel, separated by 1-unit paper keylines, corner radius 2, panel padding 8, panel radius 6. The keylines are load-bearing — they are what makes red-on-black pass as a UI boundary — and they are the 8/e register made typographic.

burtonash

Canonical colouring: red burton, amber ash. The full-width band is reserved — it appears on flagship moments, not everywhere (see 08). The one standing exception: a bar-height faceplate may sit top right in the website navigation, opposite the prompt — tiles ≈ 14 × 24, keylines intact, letters Plex Mono Bold no smaller than 11px. Below that size, switch to the three-tile ash form or drop the letters. The bar must not grow to accommodate it.

4.4 — Reserve marks

CGA colourways of the tile mark (cyan/magenta, amber-terminal trio) exist in the archive and may be used in CGA-reserve contexts only. They never replace the maynard marks.

05

Motifs

  • Register bars. Segmented colour blocks with 2-unit paper/white hairline gaps (you have seen five of them dividing this document). Use as section dividers, carousel progress markers, loading bars. Blocks draw from the core palette; max five segments.
  • Terminal chrome. Prompt lines, window title bars, inverse-video status bars. Terminal text on dark surfaces is amber or phosphor green — never red.
  • Keylined panels. Any colour block sitting on warm black takes a 1-unit paper keyline, after the 8/e faceplate.
  • Duotone photography. Photos on paper are treated as red/warm-black duotone, after the pdp11 flyer. One photo per composition, framed with a paper border on colour fields.
  • The cover recipe. One solid colour field (red or warm black), white/paper type, one framed image, tile mark small at the bottom. That is the whole layout.
  • CGA dither. The cyan/magenta checkerboard texture lives in the reserve — retro-special contexts only.

The seasoning rule: one motif per composition. If a piece has a register bar, it does not also need a terminal footer and a duotone photo.

06

Photography & editorial

The look is 1964–1978 corporate optimism: DEC's staged working scenes and the "austere futurism" of IBM's Noyes-era design programme. Professional yet stylish; the future presented as a genuinely nice place to work. This section complements the identity — it does not change it — and governs photo treatment, documents, and slides.

Photographic style

  • The machine is the hero; people make it believable. DEC's brochures showed operators mid-task in real rooms with real furniture — posed-candid, never stocky. Photograph work happening, not products floating.
  • Frontal and three-quarter compositions, tripod-steady. Panels square to the lens, symmetric where the subject allows, generous headroom. IBM shot the S/360 like architecture, because Noyes designed it like architecture.
  • Warm film palette. Kodachrome-era warmth: creams, ambers, deep reds, browns. Grade toward maynard; desaturate hues the palette does not own. Saturation belongs to the machine's panels — exactly the role the S/360's coloured doors played in a grey room.
  • Quiet backgrounds. Seamless paper, plain walls, tidy benches. Clutter is the enemy of optimism.
  • Framed on colour. On solid fields, photos sit inside a paper border — the pdp-11 brochure construction.
  • Treatments. Red/ink duotone (see 05) for graphic and cover contexts; full colour, warm-graded, for editorial interiors.
  • Shooting your own kit (the bench, the radios, the retro machines): warm directional light, hands in frame doing something real, nameplates and badges legible, one subject per frame.

Document & slide layout

  • Covers use the cover recipe (05): one solid field, paper type, one framed image, small mark. This is the pdp-11 brochure's construction, adopted whole.
  • Interiors: one disciplined grid, generous margins, Plex Sans 500 headlines set large and calm, numbered figures with mono captions. White space is a feature, not a leftover.
  • Spec tables are design elements. The datasheet is the house literary form — tables get the same care as headlines.
  • Slides: one idea per slide. Title slide = cover recipe; content slides = one figure with a mono caption, or one table; the register bar is the progress indicator.
  • Tone: confident declaratives. Optimism expressed as precision, never as exclamation marks.

Heritage notes

IBM appointed Eliot Noyes Consultant Director of Design in 1956; his programme coordinated everything from trademark and graphics to exhibitions, interiors, machines, and buildings, with Paul Rand, George Nelson, Eero Saarinen and the Eames Office contributing. The System/360 (1964) shipped with customer-chosen coloured panels — red and blue the favourites, yellow, green, white and grey also offered — and launched simultaneously in 165 cities. DEC's digital logo was designed in 1957 by Elliot Hendrickson, its boxes echoing the proportions of DEC's plug-in System Modules, its letters conceived as cut-outs showing the material beneath. In the early seventies DEC colour-coded its product lines — burnt orange and goldenrod for the PDP-8, maroon and orange for the PDP-11. maynard's structure is not merely inspired by this history; it re-inhabits it.

07

Voice

Write like the best 1976 datasheet: plain, specific, quietly funny. Explain what a thing does; never sell it. Enthusiasm shows up as precision, not exclamation marks.

08

Applications

Website

  • Theme: maynard throughout. Paper pages, warm-black body text, red for links, actions, and the nav underline.
  • Navigation: terminal prompt wordmark top left with red cursor. The tile mark is not site chrome.
  • Terminal elements (footer prompt, code blocks, the labs console) run on warm black with amber, phosphor green #339900, and paper text.
  • The faceplate band is earned, not default. It appears as a full-width warm-black band (band above the footer, or a masthead) on flagship pages only: the spec-sheet about page, series landings, launch posts. Everyday pages omit it — scarcity is what makes it read as a signature. The standing exception is the bar-height faceplate top right in the navigation (spec in 4.3), which may appear sitewide.
  • Category tags: amber (crypto), brown (radio), green-deep (retro) — mono, working weight.
  • Easter egg: the phosphor switcher (amber / green / CGA cyan) may live on terminal surfaces; cyan here is a reserve appearance and stays off page chrome.

Carousels & title cards

Use the cover recipe: red or warm-black field, paper type, one framed image or diagram, small tile mark bottom centre, register bar as the page-progress indicator. On LinkedIn this will look like nothing else on the platform, which is the point.

The spec-sheet about page

Ash is written up as a product datasheet: model no., first shipped, I/O, supported protocols, known errata. Peak "not a wanker" energy; the faceplate band is its masthead.

10

Extensions for complex implementations

Some products need more than the core system allows: cast colours, poster-scale type, diagrams with many roles. Exceptions are allowed when they follow these rules.

  • Scoped. An extension lives inside its own content surface (a demo canvas, a Stage frame, a diagram). It never leaks into site chrome, nav, footers or marks.
  • Heritage-justified. Every added colour or device has a hardware story, like everything in the core. If you can't tell the story, don't add it.
  • Same grammar. Paper and ink still carry the composition. Plex only. Lowercase marks, solid type, keylines on ink.
  • Never colour alone. Every coded role also has a shape and a text label.
  • Audited. New pairings pass WCAG AA (4.5:1 text, 3:1 shapes and UI) and are listed here before use.
  • Registered. Each extension gets a named subsection below. If it's not written here, it isn't approved.
